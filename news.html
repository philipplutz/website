  • 2 November 2026 — New team member: I’m happy to welcome Guido Priem as a post-doctoral researcher in my NWO project on social norms in immigrant societies at VU Amsterdam. Project summary
  • 24 October 2026 — New article published: My article with Sandra Lavenex and Paula Hoffmeyer-Zlotnik, “Trading migration? Explaining mobility provisions in preferential trade agreements”, is now out in The Review of International Organizations. Using the MITA dataset, we examine why about seven in ten new trade agreements facilitate the temporary mobility of businesspeople despite states’ insistence on migration control. We find that economic interdependence and the design of earlier agreements drive these provisions, while migration control concerns play little role. Link to the publication
  • 14 October 2026 — New article in print: Our research on public misperceptions about immigration is now officially published in ‘Political Science Research and Methods’. The article introduces a novel survey module to improve the measurement of inaccurate beliefs combining accuracy and confidence. The results reveal that most inaccurate survey responses are in fact uninformed bad guesses, whereas genuine misperceptions are concentrated among anti-immigrant respondents. Link to the publication
  • 1 September 2026 — Project start: Start NWO project ‘The Normative Integration Regime: How Differential Expectations Shape Immigrant Societies’ at VU Amsterdam Project description
  • 21 July 2026 — New article published: My article with Ben Crum, “Fair enough? Citizens’ preferences on refugee distribution in the EU”, is now out in the European Journal of Political Research. Using an original survey across six EU member states, we examine public preferences for distributive fairness in asylum governance. We find widespread support for European responsibility-sharing over the status quo, but show that citizens tend to favor fairness principles that minimize their own country’s refugee intake. Thus, while a transnational consensus exists on moving toward a fairer distribution, its practical operationalization reveals deeper political divides across member states. Link to the publication
  • 8 July 2026 — New article published: My article with Serena Does, “Seeing Divides: Citizens’ Biased Perceptions of Political Cleavages”, is now out in Political Behavior. We bridge cleavage theory with the study of group meta-perceptions to ask how citizens mentally map political divides. Using original survey data from Germany on immigration, we introduce a method to decompose perceptual error into three cognitive biases — egocentrism, conservatism, and false polarization — and show that these biases interact asymmetrically across the cleavage, so that the structure of citizens’ misperceptions mirrors and reinforces the structure of political conflict itself. Link to the publication
  • 7 July 2026 — New article in print: My article with Stefan Manser-Egli, “Integration for whom? The migration bias in social norms”, is now out in European Societies. In this study, we bring together critical integration research with quantitative survey research to conceptualise and test the idea of a ‘migration bias’ in social norms. Does the idea of integration come with double standards in social expectations towards natives and immigrants? Our survey experiment in Switzerland finds supportive evidence, most strongly in the social domain, and only for the German-speaking region. Link to the publication
  • 22 June 2026 — New article published: My reflection article co-authored with Diego Caballero-Velez about refugee protection as a public good in international governance is now published in the latest issue of ‘Perspectives on Politics’. We systematise the benefits that states can gain from refugee protection, and develop the argument that we should look beyond the standard rational-choice approaches and conceive of states’ understanding of refugee protection as politically constructed. Link to the publication
